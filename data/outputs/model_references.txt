Landslide Susceptibility Model -- Literature References
=======================================================
Santa Barbara County WLC Model | Ryan Green, 2026


WLC METHODOLOGY AND FACTOR WEIGHTS
------------------------------------

Dai, F.C. & Lee, C.F. (2002). Landslide characteristics and slope instability
  modeling using GIS, Lantau Island, Hong Kong. Geomorphology, 42(3-4), 213-228.
  -- Establishes slope gradient as the dominant control in terrain-based
     susceptibility models; used to justify slope receiving the highest weight (28%).

Ayalew, L. & Yamagishi, H. (2005). The application of GIS-based logistic
  regression for landslide susceptibility mapping in the Kakuda-Yahiko Mountains,
  Central Japan. Geomorphology, 65(1-2), 15-31.
  -- Widely cited GIS overlay approach; informed relative weighting of slope,
     geology, and proximity to faults.

Saha, A.K., Gupta, R.P., Sarkar, I., Arora, M.K., & Csaplovics, E. (2005).
  An approach for GIS-based statistical landslide susceptibility zonation with a
  case study in the Himalayas. Landslides, 2(1), 61-69.
  -- AHP-based weighting framework used as a structural template for assigning
     relative factor importance across the nine model inputs.

Pradhan, B. & Lee, S. (2010). Landslide susceptibility assessment and factor
  effect analysis: backpropagation artificial neural networks and their comparison
  with frequency ratio and bivariate logistic regression modelling.
  Environmental Modelling & Software, 25(6), 747-759.
  -- Compares WLC and machine learning factor rankings; used to cross-check the
     relative ordering of factors in this model.


TOPOGRAPHIC WETNESS INDEX (TWI)
---------------------------------

Beven, K.J. & Kirkby, M.J. (1979). A physically based, variable contributing
  area model of basin hydrology. Hydrological Sciences Bulletin, 24(1), 43-69.
  -- Original formulation of the Topographic Wetness Index (TWI), which underlies
     the hydrological flow accumulation factor used in this model.


SOIL ERODIBILITY
-----------------

Wischmeier, W.H. & Smith, D.D. (1978). Predicting Rainfall Erosion Losses: A
  Guide to Conservation Planning. USDA Agriculture Handbook No. 537.
  -- Defines the K-factor (soil erodibility) used as the basis for scoring
     soil units from the USDA gSSURGO database in this model.


MONTECITO DEBRIS FLOW AND THOMAS FIRE
---------------------------------------

Kean, J.W., Staley, D.M., Lancaster, J.T., Rengers, F.K., Swanson, B.J.,
  Coe, J.A., Hernandez, J.L., Sigman, A.J., Allstadt, K.E., & Lindsay, D.N.
  (2019). Inundation, flow dynamics, and damage in the 9 January 2018 Montecito
  debris-flow event, California, USA. Geosphere, 15(4), 1140-1163.
  -- Primary source for the Montecito debris flow event; used to define the
     validation target and confirm that steep, recently burned slopes were the
     principal contributing conditions.

Staley, D.M., Negri, J.A., Kean, J.W., Laber, J.L., Tillery, A.C., &
  Youberg, A.M. (2018). Prediction of spatially explicit rainfall intensity-
  duration thresholds for post-fire debris-flow generation in the western United
  States. Geomorphology, 278, 149-162.
  -- Quantifies rainfall intensity thresholds and slope-fire interaction for
     post-fire debris flow generation; informs relative weighting of slope and
     land cover in this model's fire-affected context.

Staley, D.M. et al. (2018). Post-fire debris-flow hazard assessment for the
  area burned by the 2017 Thomas Fire in Ventura and Santa Barbara Counties,
  California. USGS Open-File Report 2018-1001.
  -- Site-specific hazard assessment for the Thomas Fire burn scar; documents the
     slope thresholds and catchment characteristics that produced the Montecito
     event used as the primary validation target.


HISTORICAL LANDSLIDE INVENTORY
---------------------------------

U.S. Geological Survey (2025). Landslide Inventories across the United States,
  ver. 3.0, February 2025. ScienceBase data release.
  https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/671eef1fd34ed0f827ea9f12
  -- Source for the 1,043 historical landslide deposit polygons displayed in the
     interactive map for Santa Barbara County. Confidence field scale confirmed
     from this release: 1 = Possible, 3 = Likely, 8 = High confidence.
